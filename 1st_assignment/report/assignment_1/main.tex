{\small\textbf{Abstract.}
Turning power is compared with chapter-based predictions for constant cutting speed and constant spindle speed. Maximum pass-mean deviations are \MaxErrorVc\% and \MaxErrorN\%, respectively. Mean specific cutting pressures are \MeanKcVc\ and \MeanKcN~\unitkc. Transferring the final constant-$N$ pass estimate to drilling predicts \PrePower~kW for pre-drilling and \DrillPower~kW for full drilling, with entry ramps and a pilot-bottom power step.}

\section{Process Parameters and Predictions}
An AISI 4135 bar is turned from 48 to 24~mm over an axial length of 40~mm, using a DNMG NC5330 coated carbide tool~\sourcecite{chapter}{1}. Twelve passes machine diameters $D=46,44,\ldots,24$~mm with radial depth $a_p=1$~mm and feed $f=0.2$~mm/rev.

The programs maintain either $v_c=150$~m/min or $N=1200$~rpm. Subtracting the air-cutting trace at matching timestamps estimates cutting power:
\begin{equation}
 P_c=P_m-P_{nc},\qquad
 P_c=\frac{K_c v_c f a_p}{60},\qquad
 t_c=\frac{60L}{fN}.
 \label{eq:turning}
\end{equation}
The smallest-diameter chapter values, $K_c=3025.6$ and $3230.3$~\unitkc, respectively, are held constant across the prediction passes.

\section{Specific Cutting Pressure (Task 3)}
\subsection{Constant Cutting Speed}
For each retained sample, specific cutting pressure is calculated as
\begin{equation}
 K_c[i]=\frac{60P_c[i]}{v_c f a_p},
 \label{eq:kc}
\end{equation}
using $P_c$ in W. Each pass is summarized by its sample mean and sample SD. The implementation is linked in \code{kc_estimation_TP.py}{kc\_estimation\_TP.py}.

\reportfigure{kc_windows_constant_vc.png}{Constant-$v_c$ selection: orange dashed outlines are nominal windows beginning at detected entries and lasting the predicted duration; blue shading marks the samples retained for $K_c$. Gray is raw power and blue is the 55-sample moving average.}{fig:kcvc}

At constant $v_c$, a centered 55-sample moving average (0.495~s support) is used. Its width was selected qualitatively and iteratively relative to the shortest cutting interval; filter-tuning steps are omitted. Entries and exits are detected at 1~kW using a \MedianSamplesVc\mbox{-sample} median (approximately 0.20~s), with intervals shorter than 2~s discarded. The median serves detection only.

Each window ends at the earlier of the predicted end and detected exit. Trimming 27 samples at either boundary keeps the entire centered moving-average support inside the cut. No $K_c$-based outlier removal is applied. The early passes retain appreciable variation despite smoothing; the later retained traces are steadier.

Across the 12 pass means, the equal-weight mean is \MeanKcVc~\unitkc. The between-pass sample SD is \BetweenSdVc~\unitkc. Table~\ref{tab:kc} gives all per-pass results.

\section{Drilling Prediction (Task 4)}
The final constant-$N$ turning pass ($D=24$~mm) supplies $K_c=\LastKcN$~\unitkc\ and $v_c=\ReferenceVc$~m/min. Each drill uses this outer-edge cutting speed, giving different spindle speeds. Feed is the total axial feed per revolution; no extra cutting-lip multiplier is applied~[\hyperlink{source:slides}{2},\hyperlink{source:sandvik}{3}].

Both tools have a $120^\circ$ point angle and $f=0.2$~mm/rev. Tip depths are measured from the original surface: 10~mm for the blind conical pilot and 20~mm for the larger drill. The plotted 0.5~s interstage lag is illustrative; the 0.1~s initial padding is not cutting time.

\reportfigure{prediction_power_vs_time_drilling.png}{Predicted rotational cutting power. Entry ramps lead to full engagement. The 13~mm drill first removes an annulus, then reaches the pilot bottom and rises from \AnnularPower\ to \DrillPower~kW as the inner lips engage.}{fig:drilling}

For an engaged radial interval $[r_i,r_o]$ in mm,
\[
 M_c=\frac{K_cf(r_o^2-r_i^2)}{2000},\qquad
 P_c=M_c\frac{2\pi N}{60}.
\]
Equal point angles give simultaneous inner-lip engagement at the pilot bottom, idealized as a power step. The model omits chisel-edge effects, friction, spindle losses and withdrawal. Implementation: \code{power_prediction_DP.py}{power\_prediction\_DP.py}.

\newpage
\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
Pass means follow both predicted turning profiles (Tables~\ref{tab:comparisonvc} and~\ref{tab:comparisonn}). Cutting times agree closely at constant $N$ and are shorter than predicted at constant $v_c$ (Table~\ref{tab:timing}).

The transferred final-pass $K_c$ predicts \PrePower~kW pre-drilling and \DrillPower~kW full drilling (Table~\ref{tab:drilling}; Figure~\ref{fig:drilling}), without experimental drilling validation.
